
# Defence / Rearmament GARP Screen — 7 Names (Aug 2026 review)

  *Educational analysis, not personalized financial advice — do your own research or consult a licensed advisor. Screen originated by user (mid-2026 estimates); figures below verified against reported results as of Aug 28–30, 2026.*


## Thesis in one line

  Allied rearmament — munitions replenishment, NATO burden-shifting, missile defence (Golden Dome), and naval fragmentation — is a multi-year, budget-backed demand cycle. The GARP angle is that defence primes trade on visible, government-funded backlogs, so growth is unusually forecastable relative to the multiple paid.

## The three corrections that matter

  **1. HII is not a 14–16x stock.** This is the largest error in the screen and it inverts the ranking logic — HII was placed at #2 *because* it looked cheap. At ~21x forward it is not cheaper than NOC or LMT; it is more expensive than both. The capacity-scarcity thesis is genuinely the strongest in the group (US submarine and carrier capacity cannot be replicated — there is no second Newport News), and Q2 was excellent: revenue +10.9%, EPS $5.27 vs $3.79 consensus, shipbuilding revenue guidance raised to $10.2–10.4B. But **free cash flow was negative $150M in the quarter and −$421M for H1**, against full-year FCF guidance of only $500–600M. A shipbuilder consuming working capital while guiding to thin FCF does not deserve a GARP label on a PEG of 0.9 that doesn't exist. Also note ongoing Navy welding-quality issues and Zacks currently ranking it a #4 (Sell).

  **2. BAE and Thales are the expensive end, not the value end.** BAE at ~30x P/E with a **PEG of 4.56** is the opposite of GARP — and the market said so: despite H1 sales +9%, EPS +13%, record £84B backlog and upgraded guidance across every metric, the shares fell 8.3% after hours. That is a valuation problem, not an execution problem. Thales at ~29.6x is similar. Both are excellent businesses; neither belongs in a growth-at-a-*reasonable*-price screen at these multiples. If European exposure is wanted, the honest framing is "quality at a full price."

  **3. Leonardo's "cheapest in Europe" claim needs a caveat, not a correction.** The operating momentum is real — record €57B backlog, H1 orders +45% to €16B, guidance upgraded, and it has recovered 35%+ off its €43.15 52-week low. But the screen's "after the 2026 correction" framing is stale: the correction was the ~15% April–May drawdown around the **CEO transition (Cingolani out, Lorenzo Mariani in, via a government-backed board reshuffle)**, and the stock has since rallied to €58.47 versus a €66.26 high. Two structural items the screen omits: the Italian government owns **30.2%**, and the CEO change is a genuine industrial-continuity risk given the prior team authored the current strategy.


## Where the screen is right

  **LMT at #1 is well-supported.** ~18.3x forward against a record $230.4B backlog giving 5–7 years of cash-flow visibility, FY26 FCF guided to $7.0–7.2B, and a $35B multi-year THAAD interceptor award booked in Q2 alone. The missiles/replenishment thesis is the cleanest expression of the theme, and the multiple is the most reasonable of the large primes. One caveat the screen doesn't carry: **H1 net earnings rose 61.8% largely because prior-year reach-forward losses are absent**, so the growth rate flatters the underlying trajectory — use the guided EPS, not the YoY delta.

  **NOC may deserve to rank above #3.** Q2 delivered $20B of net awards and a **1.84x book-to-bill** against roughly 1.0x expected — the strongest order print of its cycle — yet the stock gapped down 6.5% on the day to a multi-quarter low of $479 on EAC charges and sector de-rating. At ~18.4x it trades at a ~10% discount to GD on materially better order momentum. Two dated catalysts sit ahead: the F/A-XX source selection and an Air Force decision on expanding the B-21 program of record, expected by year-end.

  **LHX's Golden Dome positioning is real, and the screen's PEG range was too wide to be useful.** Q2 revenue +8% to $5.9B, EPS $3.13 (+28%), record $42B backlog excluding roughly $25B of pending Munitions Acceleration Council orders, plus a $1B Pentagon investment to accelerate missile capacity and a PAC-3 propulsion expansion at Camden, Arkansas. The commercial space propulsion divestiture (closed August) leaves ~$4B of cash on hand. Offsetting: a **recent CEO change** (Kubasik ousted) and electronic-warfare segment softness.


## Theme-level risks the screen doesn't capture

  - **Cyclicality is political, not economic.** Every name here depends on appropriations that can be reprioritised faster than backlogs suggest. Backlog is a claim on future budgets, not a contract for them.
  - **Fixed-price contract exposure.** The regulatory drift toward fixed-price awards is what produced LMT's prior reach-forward losses and NOC's Q2 EAC charges. It transfers cost-overrun risk from government to contractor — and it is the single most reliable source of negative surprise in this sector.
  - **Labour and capacity are the real constraint.** Shipyard welders, propulsion capacity, solid rocket motors. HII, LHX and BAE are all spending to expand physical capacity; none of it converts to revenue quickly.
  - **Sector multiple compression is already underway.** LHX −16% over 90 days, HII −30.8% over 90 days, NOC to a 52-week low on a *good* print, BAE −8.3% on an upgrade. The market is de-rating the group even as fundamentals improve — which is either the opportunity or the warning, depending on whether budgets hold.
  - **Ceasefire risk.** European names in particular re-rate on conflict headlines. Barclays explicitly flagged Leonardo's low Ukraine exposure as *resilience against a ceasefire* — which tells you what the rest of the group is carrying.

## Revised read

  On verified numbers rather than the screen's estimates, the GARP ordering tightens to: **LMT and NOC** (both ~18x, both with record backlogs and the best growth-to-multiple ratios), then **GD** (~20.5x, Gulfstream diversification is a genuine hedge rather than the drag the screen implies), then **LHX** (~23–24x but the clearest Golden Dome optionality), then **HII** (best structural moat, worst near-term cash and a multiple 5–6 turns above what the screen assumed), then **Leonardo** (strong momentum, governance and state-ownership overhang), and finally **BAE and Thales**, which at ~30x and PEG above 4 do not qualify as GARP on any reading.

  The theme is sound. The main risk to acting on this screen as written is anchoring on the mid-2026 multiples in the valuation column, three of which no longer hold.
